## The problem

Passive dynamic ankle-foot orthoses (PDAFOs) support people with weakness around the ankle, but the struts that set their stiffness come in a limited range. A strut that is too stiff or too flexible changes how someone walks. My goal was a strut whose rotational stiffness could be adjusted to the user and that reaches beyond the range of motion of commercial struts.

{/* <Diagram src="/media/projects/pdafo/concept.jpg" caption="Adjustable-stiffness strut concept" size="lg" /> */}

---

## Design & analysis

I led the design of customizable, adjustable-stiffness strut geometries and verified each one with finite element analysis in **Abaqus**, checking deformation under stress and physiological loading before committing to a prototype.

## Prototyping & mechanical testing

- Machined **aluminum and steel** prototypes on the mill and lathe
- Tested them on an **Instron** system under differential loading conditions
- Correlated measured rotational stiffness with FEA predictions and published literature targets

## Testing with people

I ran verification and user-performance testing through **human subject gait analysis** in a simulated walking environment and compared the results with normative gait data.
